1. Overview of the Issue

Vehicle technology is changing, and autonomous driving systems are still developing. The policy question is how the federal government can encourage research and adoption of zero-emission autonomous vehicles without overlooking safety, affordability, and the uncertainty surrounding new technology.

Cleaner travelOne intended policy outcome of wider zero-emission vehicle adoption.
Level 3–5Higher levels of driving automation addressed by this proposal.
10 yearsProposed duration of the incentive program.

Why zero-emission vehicles?

Gasoline and diesel combustion are important sources of transportation-related carbon dioxide emissions. Battery-electric vehicles have no tailpipe emissions, although electricity generation and vehicle manufacturing can still have environmental impacts. The proposal therefore focuses on zero tailpipe-emission vehicles while recognizing that the full environmental effect depends on more than the vehicle's tailpipe.

Why autonomous vehicle research?

Automated driving systems are developing, and NHTSA identifies potential safety benefits while emphasizing the need for safe testing, validation, and oversight. NHTSA also notes that currently available consumer vehicles still require the driver's full attention; the proposal's Level 3-and-higher incentives are intended as a future-facing research and adoption policy, not a claim that fully self-driving cars are widely available today.

What is the policy gap?

Federal clean-vehicle tax credits previously provided a consumer incentive, but the IRS states that the new, previously-owned, and qualified commercial clean-vehicle credits are not available for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. This student proposal would create a new, time-limited incentive framework that specifically links zero-emission vehicle adoption with higher-level autonomous-vehicle research and development.

Important distinction: The proposal's emissions, safety, and market-share goals are policy aims—not guaranteed outcomes. Autonomous vehicle safety and environmental effects depend on technology, deployment conditions, electricity sources, and how people use the vehicles.

2. Proposal at a Glance

$3,000Proposed consumer tax credit for an eligible new zero-emission vehicle.

How it would work

  1. Support innovation: The Department of Energy (DOE) would set R&D eligibility guidelines. The IRS would administer the corporate tax credit.
  2. Support consumers: The IRS, DOE, and National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) would determine vehicle eligibility and administer purchase credits.
  3. Fund the program: A proposed $200 excise tax on purchases of new gasoline-powered vehicles would help fund a Clean Transportation Innovation Fund.
  4. Measure results: DOE, the Department of Transportation (DOT), and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) would report to Congress every two years.

The tax rates, dollar amounts, sales targets, and funding mechanism shown here are proposed provisions of this student bill; they are not descriptions of current federal law.

3. Full Text of the Bill

Representative: Dylan Yang

A BILL TO ESTABLISH INCENTIVES FOR ZERO-EMISSION AUTONOMOUS VEHICLES

Be it enacted by the House of Representatives and the United States Senate in Congress assembled, that:

Purpose & Goals

The United States should accelerate the development and adoption of zero-emission vehicles and autonomous driving technology. Electric vehicles can reduce greenhouse gas emissions, while autonomous vehicles have the potential to improve road safety and reduce human driving errors.

This legislation aims to encourage private-sector investment in zero-emission autonomous vehicle technology while making these vehicles more affordable for American consumers.

The goals of this legislation are to:

  • Increase zero-emission vehicles (ZEVs) to 50% of new light-duty vehicle sales by 2036.
  • Increase Level 3 or higher autonomous vehicles (AVs) to 10% of new light-duty vehicle sales by 2036.
  • Encourage greater corporate investment in domestic research and development (R&D) for zero-emission autonomous vehicles.

Section 1 – Definitions

  1. Electric Vehicle (EV): A vehicle propelled by one or more electric motors that draw power from an onboard electricity source.
  2. Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV): A motor vehicle that produces no tailpipe emissions of regulated air pollutants or greenhouse gases during operation.
  3. Autonomous Vehicle (AV): A vehicle equipped with an Automated Driving System (ADS) capable of performing driving tasks without continuous human control. For this legislation, vehicles must meet SAE Level 3 or higher:
    • Level 3 – Conditional Driving Automation: The vehicle can perform driving tasks under certain conditions, but a human driver must be available to take over when requested.
    • Level 4 – High Driving Automation: The vehicle performs all driving tasks within specific operating conditions without human intervention.
    • Level 5 – Full Driving Automation: The vehicle performs all driving tasks under all roadway and environmental conditions without human intervention.

Section 2 – Incentives for Vehicle Manufacturers

  1. The federal government shall establish a tax incentive program to encourage companies to invest in domestic R&D for zero-emission autonomous vehicles.
  2. Eligible companies shall receive a federal tax credit equal to 20% of qualifying domestic R&D expenditures directly related to:
    • Zero-emission vehicle batteries and energy storage.
    • Autonomous driving software and artificial intelligence.
    • Sensors and other autonomous driving technologies.
    • Technologies that improve the safety, reliability, and efficiency of zero-emission autonomous vehicles.
  3. To qualify, companies must demonstrate that their research directly contributes to the development or improvement of zero-emission vehicles equipped with Level 3 or higher autonomous driving systems.
  4. The Department of Energy (DOE) shall oversee the program, establish qualifying R&D guidelines, and evaluate participating companies.
  5. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) shall administer and distribute the corporate tax credits.

Section 3 – Incentives for Consumers

  1. The federal government shall provide tax credits to consumers purchasing eligible new zero-emission vehicles. Consumers purchasing an eligible new ZEV shall receive a $3,000 federal tax credit.
  2. Consumers purchasing eligible new autonomous vehicles shall receive an additional $2,000 tax credit, provided the vehicle meets SAE Level 3 or higher requirements.
  3. Consumers purchasing vehicles that qualify as both zero-emission and autonomous shall be eligible for the combined $5,000 tax credit.
  4. Eligible vehicles must have a manufacturer's suggested retail price (MSRP) of no more than $80,000.
  5. The IRS, in coordination with the Department of Energy and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), shall determine vehicle eligibility and administer these consumer incentives.
  6. The IRS shall allow eligible consumers to receive the credit at the point of sale, reducing the vehicle's purchase price immediately.

Section 4 – Financials and Funding

  1. To help finance this legislation, the federal government shall impose a $200 federal excise tax on the purchase of new gasoline-powered vehicles that are not zero-emission vehicles.
  2. This tax shall apply only to new vehicle purchases and shall not apply to used vehicles.
  3. Revenue collected from this tax shall be deposited into a federal Clean Transportation Innovation Fund.
  4. Congress shall allocate funding from this account toward corporate R&D tax incentives, consumer EV and AV purchase incentives, and federal administration and evaluation of the program.
  5. The Department of the Treasury, through the IRS, shall collect the excise tax and manage the distribution of funds.

Section 5 – Implementation and Impact

  1. This legislation shall remain in effect for 10 years following its enactment.
  2. The Department of Energy shall monitor corporate R&D investments and the development of zero-emission autonomous vehicle technologies.
  3. The Department of Transportation (DOT), through NHTSA, shall monitor the adoption and safety performance of autonomous vehicles.
  4. The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) shall monitor the adoption of zero-emission vehicles and estimate the resulting reductions in transportation-related greenhouse gas emissions.
  5. The DOE, DOT, and EPA shall jointly submit a report to Congress every two years evaluating the number of new EVs and AVs purchased, the amount of private-sector investment in zero-emission autonomous vehicle R&D, changes in vehicle-related emissions, and the safety performance of autonomous vehicles.
  6. Congress shall use these reports to evaluate the effectiveness of the legislation and determine whether the incentives should be extended, modified, or discontinued after the 10-year period.

Conclusion

This legislation seeks to accelerate America's transition toward cleaner and safer transportation by encouraging private-sector innovation and making emerging vehicle technologies more accessible to consumers. By combining corporate R&D incentives, consumer tax credits, and a dedicated funding mechanism, the federal government can help establish the United States as a leader in zero-emission autonomous vehicle development while addressing environmental and transportation safety challenges.

4. Letter to Representative Scott Peters

Email-style draft. Personalize the bracketed fields before sending. Use the official House contact form to submit a message to the congressional office.

To: Congressman Scott Peters
Subject: Student Bill Proposal on Zero-Emission Autonomous Vehicle Innovation

Dear Representative Peters,

My name is Dylan Yang, and I am a student from San Diego, California. I am writing to share a legislative proposal I developed for a project about the future of transportation: the Zero-Emission Autonomous Vehicle Innovation Act.

Vehicle technology is changing, and autonomous driving systems are continuing to develop. Their safe deployment will require research, testing, and oversight, while zero-emission vehicle adoption raises questions about affordability and environmental impact. I am interested in how federal policy could encourage innovation while keeping safety and consumer affordability in mind.

My proposed bill would create a 10-year program with two main approaches. First, it would offer a tax credit for qualifying domestic research and development focused on zero-emission autonomous vehicle technologies, including batteries, sensors, and autonomous driving software. Second, it would provide purchase tax credits for consumers who buy eligible zero-emission vehicles, with an additional incentive for vehicles meeting Level 3 or higher automation standards. The proposal also outlines a funding mechanism and requires federal agencies to report on investment, vehicle adoption, emissions, and safety.

I understand that autonomous vehicle technology raises important questions, including how safety should be evaluated and how incentives should be designed responsibly. I would appreciate your office's perspective on the challenges Congress should consider when evaluating policies that support research and adoption of these technologies.

Thank you for your time and for considering the ideas in my student proposal.

Sincerely,
Dylan Yang

San Diego, California
